# Creatures and NPCs

Use the entries as system-neutral benchmarks; adjust numbers to your ruleset.

## Madame Vesper, keeper of the inn

Calm, exacting, genuinely afraid. She cannot knowingly break a spoken bargain.
In conflict, she commands shadows, locks doors, and offers a bargain before
dealing harm. She knows every secret here except the party's intentions.

## Lark-of-Ash, fairy emissary

Appears as a pale adolescent with ember-bright eyes. Swift, hard to surprise,
and unable to say a direct lie. Lark can become a moth for a few heartbeats and
may grant one minor glamour to anyone who fulfills an elegantly phrased promise.

## deTour, teller of the last road

**deTour** is a human bard with rain-stained boots, a patched green coat, and a
three-string lute that never quite holds the same tuning twice. His name is his
own, distinct from *Detour* the amber draught and Détournée the amber sister: he claims
the bottle was named after a song he has not yet written. He opens the adventure
at the common-room fire, telling the party a version of the inn's history.

AC 12; HD 2; hp 8; Move 30; Attack dagger +1 (1d4); Save as 2nd-level thief;
Morale 7. He avoids violence. Once per Green Hour, if he can play loudly for one
round, mothlings within earshot retreat for 1d6 turns. Give his rumors in order,
one after each Green Hour advance: (1) the sword was made to drive a worm from a
heart; (2) the well remembers faces; (3) no oath says a champion must bleed.

## d'Aniel, hedge-wizard of the four bottles

d'Aniel is absent when the party arrives. His ink-spattered notes remain in the
bar's locked glass case, and his reflection occasionally appears late in a
mirror. He did not create the sisters' ancient gifts: he distilled and bottled
their gifts into Verte, Bleue, Crimson, and *Detour*, remedies against the
Dream-Wyrm's first foothold: fear, false paths, hungry desire, and the wish to
escape. A character who studies his notes for one turn learns one non-potion
Wyrm counter or identifies any draught without drinking it.

**Choose one truth before play.** (1) d'Aniel escaped through the dry passage;
his fifth unfinished formula in area 5 confirms the permanent-banishment route.
(2) He is trapped in the well-reflection; a character who spends the silver key
and a sincere gift frees him, and he may serve as the non-mortal substitute
champion. (3) He is the first root-double, hiding among the guests; his notes
remain useful, but he knows the party's plans through their reflections.

## The four sisters of the last pour

The sisters are not a combat encounter unless the party makes them one. Each
appears when her bottle is named, opened, or drunk; each knows one route through
the inn and one fragment of the compact. They are old enough to have witnessed
Vesper’s first bargain and divided enough to be dangerous.

### Viridienne, the green sister — Verte

Viridienne wears a coat of fern fronds and smells of wormwood after rain. She
represents **the path of revelation**: truth is worth any discomfort. She wants
the Root exposed before it is slain, because hidden evils always return.

**Offer.** Reveal every glamour, secret door, and root-double in the inn for one
turn. **Price.** The recipient must ask a question they have avoided asking.
**Clue.** “The promise says *bearing*, not *bleeding*.”
**Opposes.** Carmine's mercy when it conceals Vesper's responsibility.

### Azurée, the blue sister — Bleue

Azurée is clear-eyed, river-voiced, and crowned with frosted glass. She
represents **the path of passage**: every prison must have a way through. She
wants the road opened, even if the inn cannot remain.

**Offer.** Carry the party safely over one impossible boundary: the vanished
road, a mirror, a locked threshold, or the Root’s grasp. **Price.** Leave one
safe place behind forever. **Clue.** The Court’s reflection in the well shows a
champion carrying a sword but no body.
**Opposes.** Viridienne's demand to expose every truth before anyone may leave.

### Carmine, the red sister — Crimson

Carmine has rosehip-red hair, a thorned smile, and a pulse audible in silence.
She represents **the path of desire**: a heart that wants nothing is already
claimed by death. She wants Vesper to admit whom the compact was meant to save.

**Offer.** Restore 2d6 hp to every willing creature in the room and reveal what
each most desires. **Price.** Speak one true desire aloud. **Clue.** Vesper made
the compact to save Nella’s ancestor, the first carter lost on the moor.
**Opposes.** Detour's clever substitute if it lets Vesper evade remorse.

### Détournée, the amber sister — Detour

Détournée wears travel-stained gold and is never in the same place twice. She
represents **the path of deviation**: the chosen wrong turn that makes freedom
possible. She wants no oath fulfilled in the expected manner.

**Offer.** Open a route from the Root-chamber to any place a character has
sincerely wished to see. **Price.** Something small but beloved travels the
other way. **Clue.** A painted person, a dreamer, or a reflection can bear a
burden without carrying it in flesh.
**Opposes.** Azurée's escape if it merely moves the prison onto a new road.

## Sister concord

If all four sisters are present and hear the exact compact read aloud, they may
vote to interpret it. A majority can substitute a portrait, reflection, or dream
for the mortal champion. To win a sister’s vote, the party must either accept
her offer or advance her path’s value in play: reveal the truth (Viridienne),
free someone or something (Azurée), name a true desire (Carmine), or choose an
unexpected route (Détournée). This gives the finale a social solution that is
earned rather than discovered as a single sentence. A sister whose path is
publicly rejected changes the final scene: Viridienne reveals one painful fact,
Azurée opens one uncontrolled exit, Carmine names a desire aloud, and Détournée
moves one small beloved object to the wrong destination. These consequences are
not punishments; they make the failed argument felt in the fiction.

## Mothling

Tiny fey scavenger; weak alone, dangerous in a cloud. They extinguish lights,
steal written names, and flee from loud unmetered music. A swarm imposes
disadvantage/penalties on sight-based actions rather than dealing heavy damage.

## The Dream-Wyrm and its Pale Root

The Dream-Wyrm is an old white-and-pearl parasite that sleeps in the drowned
earth below Nowhere’s End. Its Pale Root is the part of it that can reach the
inn: a stationary fey parasite that makes one offer each Green Hour through a
sleeping or distracted character. Refusal is safe; acceptance lets it create a
root-double who knows one useful fact about the victim. Fire hurts the roots but
makes the inn unstable. Vermifuge severs a root permanently.

If the Wyrm rises, use this brief final form: AC 15; HD 6; hp 27; Move 20/burrow
20; bite +5 (2d6 and save vs. spells or dream one round); Morale 11. It is
immune to charm and illusion while its **Glamour** protection remains. Each Last
Pour boon can remove one named protection: Verte removes Glamour (no false
exits or root-doubles); Bleue removes Burrow (it cannot vanish below the floor);
Crimson removes Dread (no appearance fear save); Detour removes Escape (it
cannot retreat into a dream or mirror). Vermifuge deals 2d6 damage and forces
the Wyrm to save vs. spells or withdraw below the moor for a generation.
