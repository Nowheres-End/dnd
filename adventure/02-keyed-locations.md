# Keyed locations: Nowhere's End

## 1. Common room

**deTour's tale.** The bard **deTour** sits by the fire, tuning a three-string lute.
He greets arrivals with, “Every road has an end; this one merely misplaced its
beginning.” He knows one true but incomplete rumor and gives another after each
Green Hour advance. If asked about d'Aniel, he says the wizard bottled four ways
to survive a bad ending, then went looking for a fifth.

Read aloud: *The common room is warm enough to be suspicious. Seven travelers
sit at a long table beneath a stopped clock; no one eats. A woman in black silk
stands behind the bar, her expression arranged like a welcome.*

Madame Vesper welcomes the party, serves ordinary food, and refuses cellar talk
until someone asks for a drink by color. A character who watches the stopped
clock sees its second hand move backward once per minute. The front door opens
onto featureless mist until the compact is resolved or the Green Hour ends.

**Encounter.** The guest with leaves in her hair is Nella, a root-touched
carter. She begs the party not to let anyone sing after the thirteenth stroke.
If questioned kindly, she remembers dreaming of a white root drinking from her
mouth. If frightened or ignored at Green Hour 2, she becomes a root-double
(see `06-rules-and-encounters.md`).

**Clues.** The long table bears seven place settings but eight name-cards; the
missing name is Vesper’s. A successful listen/check at the clock hears a moth
inside its case. Nella describes a pearl-black eye opening beneath her dreams.
Asking Vesper about the empty setting makes her offer a bargain for “one harmless
service.”

## 2. The bar and cellar hatch

**d'Aniel's notes.** Behind the bottle case is a stained recipe card: *Green
shows the lie. Blue holds the road. Red remembers the living. Gold leaves a
door behind.* Studying it for one turn identifies every draught and reveals one
non-potion counter to a Dream-Wyrm protection. Use the chosen truth in
`03-creatures-and-npcs.md` to place his final clue in the dry passage, well, or
among the guests.

Four unlabelled bottles sit in a locked glass case. Vesper holds the key;
Lark-of-Ash has a silver duplicate. The hatch opens only when the clock strikes
thirteen or when a bearer speaks their true name into its keyhole.

**Treasure.** The bottles are the four Green Hour draughts. An attempt to break
the case releases green smoke: all creatures in the room must save vs. spells
or speak only in questions for one hour.

Each bottle holds three doses. Characters may drink a dose immediately or save
it for later; the Last Pour is optional and uses one dose from each chosen bottle.

**The Last Pour.** Vesper will set four clean glasses and explain the tavern
game if the party asks to drink together. Run the ritual in
`04-treasure-and-potions.md`. She watches closely: a full sincere round is the
only time all four sisters can be summoned without visiting the well.

## 3. Gallery of absent portraits

Portraits of prior innkeepers show their painted faces fading. Behind the third
portrait is a narrow service stair to area 5. Touching a portrait asks a single
question in the voice of its subject; the answer is true but costs the asker a
minor memory until sunrise. The third keeper says, “What is borne may be borne
in a frame.” This is one clue to the compact’s loophole.

**Hazard.** A character who removes a frame releases its ghost. The ghost will
answer one question about the Court, then asks to be carried outside at dawn.

## 4. The kitchen

The cook is a copper automaton with one instruction: “Feed all who arrive; feed
none who leave.” It will trade the brass cellar key for a recipe it has never
tasted. A violent solution causes it to ring the dinner bell, attracting 1d4
mothlings each Green Hour turn.

**Recipe.** “Sweetness with bitterness beneath, served cold and named for a
road.” A correct answer is *Detour*; the automaton gives the brass key and says
the gallery stair is “for staff, portraits, and regrets.”

## 5. Cask vault

Black casks form a maze. Chalk arrows point toward safety, but only arrows
drawn upside down are honest. In the central aisle, a cask marked *DETOUR*
contains a dry passage into area 6.

**Wandering danger.** On a failed navigation check or every second Green Hour
turn spent here, 1d4 mothlings descend. Burning a cask clears one route but
adds one tick to the inn’s collapse at Green Hour 5.

## 6. The whispering well

The well is full of silver moths, not water. Ask it about Vermifuge and it
answers: “A blade is only a door with an edge.” A sincere gift dropped inside
produces the silver key if the party lacks it.

**Questions.** The well answers three questions total. Ask what feeds below:
“Dreams, first; the Wyrm, last.” Ask what Vesper promised: “A bearer at the
last stroke.” Ask how a promise may be bent: “Carry what is true in what is not
flesh.”

## 7. Root-chamber

White roots fill a circular chamber around an iron chair. Vermifuge rests in
the chair's arms. Removing it wakes the Pale Root: treat it as an environment
that attacks through roots and dreams rather than a single monster.

The root-heart has AC 14, 30 hp, and is immune to ordinary edged weapons. Fire
deals double damage but advances the collapse track. Vermifuge automatically
deals 2d6 damage to it and can permanently sever one magical root each round.
While alive, the Root acts at the end of each round: restrain a creature within
20 feet (save vs. paralysis or 1d6 damage and held) or make a tempting offer.

**The Wyrm beneath.** Removing Vermifuge or reducing the Root-heart to 0 hp
breaks the ward: the floor splits and the Dream-Wyrm rises. Before Green Hour
6 it has only one action each round; at Green Hour 6, or after three accepted
Root offers, it has two. The Last Pour’s four saving boons are vermifuges: each
spent against the Wyrm removes one of its protections in the finale. See the
specific protections and resolution order in `06-rules-and-encounters.md`.

**Compact loophole.** The Court is owed “a mortal champion bearing the blade.”
An intelligent creature who is not mortal may bear it; so may a mortal only in
a painting, reflected image, or dream. The party can use this to break the
cycle without sacrificing a character.

**Moon-wormwood.** Three silver-veined wormwood fronds grow through the roots
behind the chair. They bloom when a character asks a feared question aloud or
the party releases a root-double; cutting them by force makes the Root offer a
temptation immediately. See the harvest and distillation procedure in
`04-treasure-and-potions.md`.
