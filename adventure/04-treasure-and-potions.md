# Treasure and potions

## Vermifuge

An old black sword with a pale bone grip. It is a potent weapon against fey,
plant creatures, parasites, and enchantments that bind a person to a place.
Once each Green Hour, it can cut a magical bond, locked route, or root. Each
use whispers the name of a nearby thing it wishes to kill; the bearer need not
obey.

**Cost.** A bearer who draws Vermifuge against an innocent sees harmless roots
in that person's shadow until the next dawn.

## The four Green Hour draughts

Each dose is a fictional magical consumable. The collection takes inspiration
from the product names and broad public botanical themes of Nowhere's End
Absinthe; all game mechanics are original.

Each bottle is also a door to one of the four faerie sisters. d'Aniel distilled
their gifts into mortal glass; he did not create the sisters or their paths.
Naming the color
and pouring a dose into a clean glass summons that sister for one exploration
turn. A sister will not appear if her bottle has been destroyed; the party can
still use its ordinary potion effect.

**Drinking at any time.** Each bottle holds three doses. A character may drink
one dose whenever they can take an action, whether in exploration, a fight, or
at an ordinary tavern. Resolve its effect immediately and apply its price; this
does not require the other bottles, the sisters, or the Last Pour. Mark each
dose used. A bottle is empty only after its third dose.

### Moon-wormwood: the Verte herb expedition

The green bottle at the inn is a nearly spent sample, not a reward that makes
the adventure's herb quest irrelevant. To replenish it, the party needs three
sprigs of **moon-wormwood**, a silver-veined plant growing in the Root-chamber.
The route is the service stair, cask vault, whispering well, and Root-chamber;
the party can learn it from the third portrait, kitchen automaton, or
upside-down chalk arrows. This gives the herb three independent routes in.

Moon-wormwood blooms when someone publicly asks a question they truly do not
want answered, or when the party frees a root-double rather than killing it.
Either choice is enough. It may also be cut by force, but then the cutter speaks
only in questions until the next dawn and the Root gets one immediate offer.
Once it blooms, harvesting three sprigs takes one turn and no roll. d'Aniel
(or a competent alchemist) can refill one three-dose Verte bottle during a
quiet rest. This makes the herb a visible choice with a price, not a treasure
hidden behind a check.

| Draught | Effect | Price |
|---|---|---|
| **Verte** | Reveals concealed doors, glamours, and green growing things for one exploration turn. | The drinker speaks only in questions until they rest. |
| **Bleue** | Lets the drinker walk across still water, mirrors, or reflected paths. | Their reflection acts one beat late until dawn. |
| **Crimson** | Restores vigor and lets the drinker hear a creature's strongest desire. | A red flower blooms wherever they sleep next. |
| **Detour** | Opens a brief route to any place the drinker has sincerely wished to see. | Something small but valued arrives at the old destination instead. |

**The fifth pour.** If all four draughts are poured, untouched, into the
whispering well, the sisters appear together. This is the surest way to learn
that the compact can be interpreted rather than simply obeyed.

## The Last Pour

The bottles can be drunk freely; Madame Vesper calls the special shared ritual
**the Last Pour**. The sisters call it a
*vermifuge*: a ritual that drives the Wyrm from the mind before it reaches the
heart. It can be played once per Green Hour by two or more willing drinkers,
with each choosing a different draught. Missing cups may be taken by a willing
NPC, a released portrait ghost, or one character who drinks a second cup and
takes both prices. The game is safe for ordinary guests, though no one leaves
it unchanged.

1. Each player names a thing they hope to find before they drink. No one may
   choose the same draught twice.
2. In order—Verte, Bleue, Crimson, Detour—each **character** states one
   question, one road, one desire, or one wrong turn they regret. Players may
   veil an answer or pass without explanation; a passing character receives
   only the potion’s ordinary effect.
3. For every sincere answer, the corresponding sister appears and grants her
   offer without requiring its usual price. Record the answer on the table.
4. If all four answers are given, the sisters name one shared truth about the
   current danger. At Nowhere’s End, it is: “No oath says the champion must
   bleed.” The four sisters are then present for concord.

### The sisters’ cups

The Last Pour’s boon lasts until the next dawn or until used. It applies before
the roll; in a game without advantage, grant +2 to the stated saving throw.

| Cup | Tavern boon |
|---|---|
| **Verte** | Once, turn a failed save against a glamour, charm, illusion, or Root offer into a success. |
| **Bleue** | Once, succeed on a save against being restrained, trapped, drowned, or rooted in place. |
| **Crimson** | Once, reroll a failed save against fear, poison, death, or parasitic loss of vitality. |
| **Detour** | Once, treat a failed save against a curse or Root effect as a partial success: suffer half effect and appear in the nearest plausible safe space. |

If the referee and player agree that a character has deliberately contradicted
an established in-world fact, their boon instead nourishes the Dream-Wyrm: it
gains +2 on its next saving throw or resistance check. Never test or demand a
player's real-life truth; the game remembers only what the character has said.

**Tavern stakes.** The first *character* to laugh must reveal a minor in-world
secret. If the referee establishes a lie, the drinker takes the potion’s price
twice; never require a player to disclose personal information. A character who
answers honestly may ask another character one question; that character may
answer, veil the answer, or forfeit their draught’s boon. The game creates table
drama without pressuring the people playing it.

**Outside the module.** In an ordinary tavern, use the game as a character
introduction or rumor engine. Each complete round gives the group one shared
rumor, invitation, or route toward the next adventure; its truth is up to the
referee.

### Other treasure

- Silver key: opens the cellar hatch and any lock made under a fairy bargain.
- Four portrait frames: each is worth modest coin, but removing one releases
  its prior keeper as a helpful, melancholy ghost.
- Moth-crown: a delicate circlet worth significant coin to a collector; worn
  at dusk, it lets its wearer understand fairy etiquette for one hour.
