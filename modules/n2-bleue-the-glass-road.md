# N2: Bleue - The Glass Road

**For 4-6 characters of levels 3-4.** A system-neutral fantasy adventure for
one long session or two short ones.

## What this module promises

The village of Bellwether has disappeared from every map except its own well.
At moonrise, its people can be seen walking across the reflected surface of the
dry River Sel. They will reach the far shore as shadows unless the party gets
them through the **Glass Road**. To distill Bleue, d'Aniel needs **glass fennel**,
which grows only where a traveler chooses to cross while leaving something
behind.

The procedure is a route puzzle, not a sequence of checks: players choose
which travelers, truths, and possessions may cross each of three boundaries.

## Run this first

Read aloud: *The riverbed is white dust. In its empty channel lies a second
night sky, black and deep enough to reflect the moon. Along that false water,
small figures walk east with their heads bowed. None of them leaves a mark.*

- Put a seven-box **Dawn track** where everyone can see it. Advance it after
  each journey turn or loud delay. At 4, shadows can speak; at 6, the ferry
  leaves; at 7, each unrescued villager becomes a reflection.
- Give the players three obvious clues: the well shows a bridge only in
  reflection; every village door has a missing nameplate; a blue moth rests on
  a compass needle pointing down.
- State the crossing price before the party crosses a boundary. Players may
  negotiate the price, but the Ferryman never conceals it.

## Referee facts

1. The Glass Ferryman erased Bellwether after its mayor tried to make a road
   that took tolls but paid no price.
2. Azurée, the Bleue Muse, wants every person freed; the Ferryman wants one
   named memory to anchor the road.
3. Glass fennel sprouts at the **third crossing**, where a willing traveler
   leaves a true possession and walks on without it.
4. The mayor, Elian Wold, remembers the bargain and is hiding among his own
   shadow-people. He would rather sacrifice the town's history than confess.
5. The party can save the village, preserve the road, or expose the mayor.
   They cannot accomplish all three without making an unusually clever new
   bargain.

## The glass-road procedure

Each journey turn, the party chooses a route and one lead traveler. A sensible
plan succeeds; call for a difficult check only when risk is uncertain. Failure
adds a complication, never a dead end. A Bleue dose lets a character cross a
boundary but does not choose the price for them.

| Boundary | Visible problem | Three fair solutions | Price if crossed directly |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. The dry ford | The reflected party walks below the dust. | Follow the well's image; walk backward while naming a destination; ask a villager to lead. | Leave a direction behind: the group cannot use its compass until dawn. |
| 2. The reed gate | Reeds repeat a person's most convenient lie. | Speak the lie aloud and revise it; burn a written lie; use Verte to reveal the honest channel. | One traveler loses a minor cherished memory until the finale. |
| 3. The ferry wake | A silver ferry crosses no water. | Pay with an object freely given; take the underwater footpath; convince the Ferryman a reflection can pay. | Leave a true possession on the far bank. Glass fennel blooms here. |

## Keyed locations

### 1. Bellwether square

Six empty houses surround a well. The town ledger lists every resident except
Elian Wold. Searching the mayor's office finds a river-toll contract with the
last page torn out. A child-shadow, Mina, asks the party to take her wooden
horse across; she is a reliable guide if treated as a person rather than a
clue.

### 2. The mirror well

Looking into the well shows the Glass Road at its current state. Asking a
question costs a dropped coin and gives one true answer: *The road eats names*;
*the ferry is on time*; or *the herb grows where the hand opens.* Breaking the
well releases 1d4 hostile reflections but also reveals the underwater footpath.

### 3. Dry ford

Use Boundary 1. A drowned knight-reflection offers to carry a character across
in exchange for their name. It means a name spoken only at introductions, not
their identity; state this distinction if the players ask. The knight knows the
mayor was present for the original compact.

### 4. Reed gate

Use Boundary 2. The gate contains the mayor's lie: "A road belongs to whoever
builds it." If the party returns the missing final page of the contract here,
the reeds part without a price and the Dawn track does not advance.

### 5. Silver ferry

Use Boundary 3. The Ferryman is courteous, literal, and exhausted. AC 14; HD
4; oar +4 (1d6, target is moved 20 feet); morale 7. He will not fight if a
better payment is offered. His true want is to be remembered by one living
person after the road closes.

### 6. The third crossing

Glass fennel grows through the planks in clear blue fronds. It can be cut only
after a willing creature puts down a true possession and steps away. The plant
contains three usable sprigs. Cutting it by force makes it ordinary fennel and
draws 2d4 mirror eels; finding a voluntary gift remains possible afterward.

### 7. East shore / the name-house

The missing villagers wait in a house whose walls are made of nameplates. Here
Elian admits the original deal: he erased the town from maps so traders would
have to use his toll road. The Ferryman enforced a bargain he did not write.
The party decides which fact is carried back.

## Factions and pressures

| Faction | Wants | Offer | Pressure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Azurée | every villager crosses | a route over any boundary | calls the party cowardly if it abandons anyone |
| Ferryman | one lasting memory | safe ferry passage | leaves at Dawn 6 |
| Elian Wold | his guilt hidden | the contract's torn page | trades another person's memory first |
| Bellwether villagers | home and names | guides, local knowledge | become reflections at Dawn 7 |

## Finale: decide what the road remembers

At the name-house, give the party all three choices in plain language.

1. **Free Bellwether.** Return every name. The road collapses; the Ferryman is
   forgotten unless a character speaks a promise to remember him. Gain the
   glass fennel and a Bleue bottle with three doses.
2. **Keep the road.** Give it Elian's name and guilt. Bellwether returns, but
   the mayor becomes the new Ferryman. The road remains usable once per year.
3. **Rewrite the toll.** Have a reflection carry the contract, a character
   promise public memory of the Ferryman, and Elian confess at the square.
   This takes a risky group check or an earned sister boon; on failure, choose
   which part breaks rather than declaring the attempt impossible.

## The Bleue draught

With glass fennel, d'Aniel distills three doses in his between-adventure
stillroom. A dose lets a drinker cross
still water, a mirror, or one impossible boundary. Until dawn, their reflection
acts one beat late. A dose may be taken whenever the drinker can act.

## Playability checks

- Three clues point to each secret: well/ledger/knight for the bargain;
  contract/reeds/Elian for the mayor; Ferryman/well/third crossing for the herb.
- Every boundary has three solutions plus player invention.
- The party sees the price before it pays it.
- The finale has three materially different endings and no mandatory combat.

## Harvest procedure: glass fennel

At the third crossing, show the clear blue fronds before asking for a payment.
Any willing traveler may leave a true possession, step away, and cause three
sprigs to bloom. A true possession means an object with real in-world history,
not coin bought for the purpose; let the player define why it matters. If the
party refuses the direct price, they may use the underwater path, a reflection,
or a new bargain with the Ferryman. In each case the herb remains visible and
harvestable once a voluntary leaving occurs; no roll or hidden room gates it.

## The Blue Sister at the table

**Azurée** wears river-glass boots and keeps the names of every traveler she
has guided inside a blue bottle. Her line is, "A crossing has a price; a theft
has a victim." Once per session, she states the fair price of a route before
the party risks it. She cannot pay it for them or make it painless.

## Bottle play: the Blue Hour

Put three blank circles beside the Bleue bottle. When a character drinks, ask:
**What true possession does your reflection envy?** The player may answer,
decline, or name an invented possession. Either way the dose functions; an
answer lets the late reflection point to one honest crossing. The Ferryman's
tavern game is *Leave / Carry / Return*: each player names one thing a traveler
should leave, carry, or return. No real alcohol is involved.
