# N3: Crimson - The Thorn Feast

**For 4-6 characters of levels 4-5.** A social dungeon and midnight ritual for
one long session or two short ones.

## What this module promises

The Briar Duke sends invitations that grant each guest a private wish before
collecting its price at midnight. The party enters the moving Thorn Hall to
rescue a guest they care about, expose the Duke's bargain, and harvest **rose
anise** from the heart-garden. Carmine, Muse of Crimson, insists that desire
named honestly is less dangerous than desire hidden.

This is a party with consequences, not a scene of compulsory seduction. Every
temptation has a stated benefit, cost, and way to decline.

## Run this first

Read aloud: *Your invitation bears a wax rose and a sentence in your own hand:
Tonight, you may ask for what you truly want. The gate opens onto music, candle
smoke, and thorns climbing a house too large to fit behind it.*

- Put a six-box **Midnight track** on the table. Advance after every scene,
  loud fight, or prolonged debate. At 3, wishes begin coming true; at 5, the
  masks fuse; at 6, the Duke collects.
- Ask every player for a desire their character could admit in public. A player
  may pass or write it privately; never demand personal disclosure.
- Give each character a black invitation token. Spending it lets them ask one
  guest a direct question without etiquette penalty.

## Referee facts

1. The Duke feeds on desires that people deny, not desires they state.
2. The guest of honor, Marrow Vale, accepted a cure for their sibling in return
   for hosting the collection. Marrow has not told the sibling.
3. Rose anise flowers at the heart-garden only when a guest names a desire and
   refuses the Duke's easy version of it.
4. Carmine wants the truth public; the Duke wants bargains private. Both can
   keep promises, but only Carmine offers a price before the vow is spoken.
5. The house is held together by three masks: Hunger, Devotion, and Ambition.
   Removing or changing each changes the finale; none requires combat.

## The feast procedure

On each scene, choose a room, an NPC, and an etiquette move: **toast** (ask a
truth), **dance** (move somewhere impossible), **gift** (trade an advantage),
or **refusal** (take social heat to reject a demand). A sensible roleplayed
move works. A failed difficult check introduces a complication: an unwanted
wish, a rival's attention, or one Midnight tick.

| Mask | Where it appears | What it promises | How to change it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hunger | the endless buffet | a lost thing returned | share food with a hungry guest before taking any |
| Devotion | the rose ballroom | love without uncertainty | dance while saying what cannot be promised |
| Ambition | the candle library | victory without cost | give credit for a success to someone else |

Each changed mask gives a **red witness**: once, a character may reroll a save
against fear, poison, death, or parasitic vitality loss.

## Feast state: use this every scene

Put three boxes marked **Heat**, three boxes marked **Rose-mark**, and three
cards marked **Rival** on the table. Heat belongs to the group; rose-marks
belong to the character who accepted a private wish.

- **Heat 1:** a rival shadows the next scene. **Heat 2:** the Duke learns one
  useful detail and offers it back at a price. **Heat 3:** the active mask fuses
  to its owner; that owner will not help until the mask is changed.
- A **thorn token** is a stated boundary: spend it to refuse one etiquette
  demand without gaining Heat, then say what line the character will not cross.
  A character gains one thorn token the first time they name a desire or price
  aloud and decline the Duke's easy version; no character can hold more than one.
- At three **rose-marks**, the marked character becomes a willing collector at
  midnight. Another character may remove one mark by publicly naming the
  marked character's real desire and refusing its easy fulfillment.
- When a **Rival** card appears, choose the guest with the most to lose in the
  current room. They offer an immediate practical favor for a declared later
  concession; they do not make a hidden demand.

## Keyed locations

### 1. Gate of invitations

The gate asks each entrant what they want. A truthful answer opens it; a false
one opens it too, but the house repeats the lie later. A servant, Pella, warns
that the Duke never forces a signature—he makes people grateful to sign.

### 2. Endless buffet (Hunger)

Every dish is a memory someone misses. Eating restores 1d6 hp but gives the
Duke a detail he may use in conversation. The missing sibling's cure is here:
real, effective, and already fading. Sharing a plate with Pella or another
hungry guest changes the Hunger mask.

### 3. Rose ballroom (Devotion)

Pairs dance on paths of thorn. A dancer may reach any room when the music ends.
An estranged couple, Iven and Sera, have both been offered perfect versions of
the other. If they speak honestly they help change Devotion; if separated, they
become polite thorn-statues at Midnight 5.

### 4. Candle library (Ambition)

Books contain futures written in flattering prose. A reader gains a useful true
fact but must name who deserves credit for the next victory. The Duke's ledger
proves Marrow invited their sibling knowingly. Giving Pella credit for a clue,
or publicly crediting another player character's plan, changes Ambition.

### 5. Private conservatory

The Duke offers each character a wish with exact terms. Read the benefit and
price before the player decides. A wish may solve a practical problem, but it
also adds a rose-mark; three rose-marks make the character a willing collector
at midnight unless another character calls their true desire by name.

### 6. Heart-garden

Rose anise grows on a thorned trellis around a sleeping stone heart. It blooms
when a guest names a desire and rejects the Duke's cheap fulfillment. Three
flowers can be cut without harm. Cutting one by force causes a thorn guardian
(AC 15; HD 5; 2 vines +5, 1d6 and restrained; morale 9) to awaken, but a later
honest refusal still lets the herb bloom.

### 7. High table

At Midnight, the Duke sets three signed bargains before the party: Marrow's,
the sibling's unwitting promise, and the guest who has taken the most wishes.
He is AC 15; HD 6; thorn rapier +6 (1d8); he cannot use a social power against
someone who has named their desire aloud. He bargains before fighting.

## Finale: what desire is owed?

1. **Public accounting.** Present the ledger, changed masks, and witnesses.
   The Duke must void bargains that were not informed. Marrow may still choose
   to pay personally. This saves the sibling and breaks the hall's glamour.
2. **Steal the heart.** Take the heart-stone through the ballroom's impossible
   route. The house survives as an angry recurring location; the Duke loses his
   leverage tonight. This works even if nobody trusts Carmine.
3. **Write a better bargain.** Give the Duke honest desires from three willing
   people, no one carrying three rose-marks, and a future service that players
   define. The Duke releases the guests but gains a clearly stated later claim.

## The Crimson draught

Rose anise lets d'Aniel make three Crimson doses. A dose restores 2d6 hp or
reveals a creature's strongest present desire. Wherever the drinker next sleeps,
a red flower blooms. It can be drunk whenever the drinker can act.

## Playability checks

- The ledger, Pella, and every mask reveal the Duke's method.
- The herb has an ethical trigger, a forceful fallback, and no hidden roll gate.
- Characters may refuse every wish and still reach all endings.
- The three finales resolve rescue, power, and future-cost play differently.

## Factions and clue web

| Faction | Wants | Reveals |
|---|---|---|
| Carmine | desire named publicly | rose anise blooms through honest refusal |
| Briar Duke | bargains made privately | a wish always has a price he will state |
| Marrow Vale | the sibling cured without exposure | the Duke's ledger is in the candle library |
| Pella and the guests | an informed choice | every mask points to one of the Duke's methods |

Three independent clue paths reveal the garden condition: Carmine, the masks,
and Pella. Three clue paths reveal Marrow's deal: the ledger, the buffet's cure,
and the Duke's private offer. Deliver a clue as the consequence of engagement,
not as payment for a successful die roll.

## The Red Sister at the table

**Carmine** wears red gloves and a thorn halo; she laughs only at the Duke,
never at a refusal. Her line is, "Name the price before you admire the gift."
Once per session, she lets a character require an NPC to state the cost of a
bargain before hearing its reward. She will not protect a character from a
price they knowingly accept.

## Bottle play: the Red Hour

Put three blank circles beside the Crimson bottle. When a character drinks,
ask: **What desire will you name without demanding its fulfillment?** A player
may pass or state a fictional answer. The magical effect always works; a named
desire creates a red witness usable once to reroll a save against fear, poison,
death, or parasitic vitality loss. The feast's tavern game is *Toast / Refuse /
Counteroffer*: one player offers a made-up bargain and another names a fairer
term. This is fictional social play, not a demand for intimacy.

## Social scene control sheet

At the start of every feast scene, put one guest in the active room and show
their **want**. The party chooses a room, one guest, and one etiquette move:
toast, dance, gift, or refusal. Resolve the chosen move, then apply the result
below and advance Midnight only after the scene has changed.

| Move | Immediate gain | Social cost | Next visible change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Toast | ask the active guest one direct question | the Duke learns one useful detail | a rival arrives with an offer |
| Dance | move the pair to any known room | accept a thorn token or name a limit | another guest follows the music |
| Gift | trade an advantage or secret | lose the offered thing until dawn | the receiver states a favor openly |
| Refusal | break a demand or leave a bargain unsigned | take one social heat | a mask begins to crack or fuse |
