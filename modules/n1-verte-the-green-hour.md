# N1: Verte - The Green Hour

**For 4-6 characters of levels 2-3.** An inn mystery and cellar expedition for
one long session or two short ones.

## What this module promises

At the thirteenth stroke, Nowhere's End loses its road and its guests begin
growing second shadows. The only cure is **moon-wormwood**, a silver-veined herb
in the cellar roots. Viridienne, the green Muse, offers revelation; Madame
Vesper offers safety; the Pale Root offers answers that should not be trusted.

This is the start of the Four Muses line. The party can earn a three-dose
Verte bottle by finding and harvesting the herb. The Dream-Wyrm is glimpsed as
the Root's distant master, not fought to a final conclusion here.

## Run this first

Read aloud: *The inn's clock chimes thirteen. Every lamp turns green. Outside,
the road has become a hedge of silver moths. A woman at the bar says, very
carefully, "No one needs to go below. No one needs to ask what is growing."*

- Put a six-box **Green Hour track** where everyone can see it. Advance it
  after each exploration turn, loud fight, or extended delay. At 2, one guest
  becomes a root-double; at 4, the cellar begins to flood with white roots; at
  6, the Pale Root offers one character a permanent bargain.
- Show three immediate pressures: Nella's shadow is moving independently, a
  fairy emissary wants the cellar key, and the exit road is gone.
- State every bargain's price before it binds. The Root may lie by omission but
  never by a directly stated falsehood.

## Referee facts

1. Madame Vesper has hidden the cellar because the Pale Root feeds on secrets
   guests refuse to name.
2. Moon-wormwood grows behind the iron chair only when a feared question is
   asked aloud or a root-double is released instead of destroyed.
3. Viridienne wants the compact exposed; Vesper wants it contained; Lark-of-
   Ash wants the Root severed quietly.
4. Vermifuge can cut magical roots, but taking it wakes the Root and begins the
   finale. It does not need to be taken to harvest the herb.
5. The whispering well, d'Aniel's recipe card, and the portrait gallery each
   reveal the herb's bloom condition.

## The Green Hour procedure

At the end of a turn, describe one visible sign before advancing the track:
green condensation, moths at a keyhole, a guest repeating a sentence, or a
root lifting a floorboard. Escalation narrows time, not player options.

| Green Hour | Event | What remains possible |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hatch unlocks; glamours thin. | Every route is open. |
| 2 | Nella becomes a root-double unless helped. | The double may still be freed. |
| 3 | Lark demands the cellar key. | He will trade a silver duplicate. |
| 4 | Roots close one ordinary exit. | The service stair and well remain routes. |
| 5 | Vesper offers the compact. | Her ledger is now available for a stated price. |
| 6 | The Root makes its final offer. | Harvest, bargain, or cut roots; never end play on a refusal. |

## Keyed locations

### 1. Common room

Vesper serves food while avoiding direct cellar questions. Nella sees a pearl
eye under the floor; deTour sings that "what grows below hears what is hidden."
Ask each character which guest they notice first. A character who speaks a
secret of their own gains a true rumor from deTour: the herb blooms for a
question that hurts to ask.

### 2. Bar and hatch

The hatch is locked. Vesper has its key; Lark has a silver duplicate; the brass
key hangs in the kitchen automaton's chest. d'Aniel's recipe card names
moon-wormwood and says: *Cut only after the root hears mercy or truth.*

### 3. Portrait gallery

The third portrait hides the service stair. Touching any portrait gives one
true answer but costs a minor memory until dawn. The gardener portrait says,
"A green thing opens when it is seen." A painted root-double can be released
by naming its original owner; it teaches the safe cask-vault route.

### 4. Kitchen

A copper automaton trades the brass key for a recipe it has never tasted:
bitterness beneath sweetness, served cold, and named for a color. The answer is
Verte. Violence rings a bell that adds one Green Hour tick, but the party can
also pick, steal, bargain, or use Lark's duplicate.

### 5. Cask vault

Black casks form a maze. Upside-down chalk arrows are honest. A failed route
check costs a turn and draws 1d4 mothlings; careful mapping, the portrait's
advice, or a Verde-like reveal avoids the check entirely. The dry cask passage
reaches the whispering well.

### 6. Whispering well

The well holds silver moths, not water. A sincere gift earns one true answer:
*the herb grows where the root is forgiven*; *the Root feeds on unasked
questions*; or *the sword is a door with an edge.* Breaking the well releases
moths but opens a direct crawl to the chamber.

### 7. Root-chamber

White roots surround an iron chair holding Vermifuge. Behind it is a closed
bud of moon-wormwood. The Root-heart is AC 14, 30 hp; ordinary edged weapons
do not harm it, fire deals double damage but advances the track, and Vermifuge
deals 2d6. The Root can restrain a creature within 20 feet (save vs paralysis
or 1d6 damage and held) or offer a fact in return for a secret.

## Harvesting moon-wormwood

The herb blooms when the party does one of the following:

- asks a feared question aloud, including one about Vesper, a root-double, or
  a character's stated past;
- releases Nella or another root-double instead of treating it as a monster;
- gives the Root a true secret and then refuses its offered bargain.

Once it blooms, any character can cut three fronds in one turn without a roll.
Cutting the closed bud by force works, but the cutter speaks only in questions
until dawn and the Root acts immediately. No route hides the herb behind a
single check.

## Factions

| Faction | Wants | Will offer | Will not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Viridienne | revelation | show a hidden path | conceal Vesper's bargain |
| Vesper | containment | food, lodging, her ledger | freely open the hatch |
| Lark-of-Ash | a quiet severing | silver key, fairy etiquette | say the Court's forbidden name |
| Pale Root | unasked truths | clues, false safety | state a direct lie |

## Finale: what is exposed?

1. **Harvest and leave.** Take the herb, free who can be freed, and escape by a
   route the party earned. The Root remains a later threat, but Verte is made.
2. **Sever the feeding root.** Use Vermifuge or fire. The Root recoils and
   releases its doubles; the Wyrm is foreshadowed in the collapsing floor.
3. **Rewrite Vesper's secrecy.** Make Vesper publicly name the compact while
   Viridienne witnesses. The Root loses its food for a season; Vesper owes the
   party one clear answer in a future module.
4. **Take the Root's bargain.** Accept one stated secret in exchange for a
   safe harvest. The party gets Verte but the Root gains a named future claim.

## The Verte draught

d'Aniel distills three doses from moon-wormwood in his between-adventure
stillroom. A dose reveals concealed
doors, glamours, and living roots for one exploration turn. Until the drinker
rests, they speak only in questions. A dose may be drunk whenever the drinker
can act.

## Playability checks

- The hatch has three keys and five ways below; no single NPC is mandatory.
- The bloom condition has three clue sources and three ways to satisfy it.
- Green Hour escalation remains visible and never deletes all exits.
- Four endings make combat, mercy, exposure, and bargain all viable play.

## Clue web

| Truth | Three clues |
|---|---|
| Where the herb grows | recipe card; gardener portrait; whispering well |
| What makes it bloom | recipe card; deTour's song; a freed root-double |
| How to reach the cellar | Vesper's key/Lark's duplicate; portrait stair; kitchen automaton |

Give a clue whenever the party follows an obvious lead; never make one of these
facts depend on a single roll.

## The Green Sister at the table

**Viridienne** is a small fairy in a moth-wing veil, carrying a bottle whose
label changes whenever someone tells the truth. Her line is, "Ask it where
everyone can hear." Once per session, after a player names a truth, she reveals
one concealed door, false face, or safe route. She will not choose whom to
expose for the party.

## Bottle play: the Green Hour

Put three blank circles beside the Verte bottle. A character may drink a dose
at any time they can act. Before resolving it, ask: **What are you afraid will
be noticed?** The player may answer, decline, or answer in character-fiction
only. An answer gives a sensory clue about the nearest secret; a decline never
withholds the potion's stated effect. At the bar, deTour offers a harmless game:
name a question the character would never ask. The first such question asked
aloud during the Green Hour grants the group one free Green Hour pause.

## Fast room roster

| Area | First visible fact | Immediate action | If violence starts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Common room | Nella's shadow moves first | comfort, question, or follow it | guests scatter; advance the track |
| Bar/hatch | Vesper guards the key | bargain, steal, or seek a second key | the hatch stays locked, not inaccessible |
| Gallery | portraits whisper names | ask a portrait or find the stair | one minor memory is lost until dawn |
| Cask vault | arrows point upside-down | map, follow, or force a route | 1d4 mothlings arrive |
| Root-chamber | Vermifuge holds the chair | harvest, bargain, or sever | Root-heart restrains a nearby creature |

## Vermifuge: double-dragon sword

Vermifuge is a jade-veined blade with a paired black-dragon guard and a
thorn-bound grip. It is a **key before it is a weapon**. It cuts magical roots,
severs one Wyrm protection in N4, and deals 2d6 to the Root-heart. Whenever it
cuts an enchantment, its bearer sees the bargain that made it. On a complication,
that vision gives the foe one useful truth about the bearer; never impose a
hidden curse. The blade's dragons face outward: it protects a road only when
its bearer lets someone else choose their own direction.
