# A+ Playability Gate: The Four Muses of Nowhere's End

This is the release bar for every module in the line. A module is not called
finished because it has evocative prose or attractive art; it must let a
referee start, improvise, and finish a satisfying session without inventing
missing structure.

## Required table evidence

| Requirement | Evidence required in each module | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate situation | a read-aloud opening plus 2-3 visible pressures | players can act before lore arrives |
| Time pressure | named public escalation track | pacing is transparent rather than punitive |
| Herb expedition | herb name, location, bloom/harvest rule, and potion output | the absinthe inspiration is play, not decoration |
| Fair access | at least three clue paths or alternatives to the key objective | one missed room or failed roll cannot end the adventure |
| Declared prices | bargains and shortcuts state benefit and cost first | player choice is informed |
| Factions | at least three factions with conflicting wants | social play creates non-combat solutions |
| Exploration aid | numbered referee route map | the referee can present space and routes reliably |
| At-table aid | dashboard with track, herb, dose, finale menu | no page hunting in a tense scene |
| Action roster | a concise room, crossing, guest, or finale roster | the next live decision is visible at a glance |
| Muse presence | named sister, voice, intervention, and refusal boundary | the four paths are characters, not labels |
| Bottle play | an optional fictional pour question that never gates the dose | potions recur in play without pressuring disclosure |
| Player card | a printable dose tracker with effect and pouring prompt | the bottle can be used without handing the referee booklet around |
| Map procedure | a start, decision loop, and climax key on the map page | route diagrams become runnable scenarios |
| Player boundary | a player-facing handout with only opening information | mystery stays playable without secret leakage |
| Meaningful ending | at least three different end states | combat is a choice, not a default answer key |

## Campaign evidence

- The four potion effects are distinct and useful beyond their originating
  adventure.
- Each potion has a strange fictional price and is never presented as real
  beverage or health guidance.
- The rules shorthand is explicitly d20-compatible rather than falsely
  system-neutral: convert AC, HD, morale, and saves to the table's own game.
- A group may play any module alone. N4 accepts missing prior bottles through
  equivalent earned support, preventing campaign-resource lockout.
- The bottle palettes, cover structure, typography, maps, and player handouts
  remain visually consistent while the adventure engines differ.
